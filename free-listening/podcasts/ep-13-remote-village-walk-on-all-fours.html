Remote village where people walk on all fours

B2 🧬 Science 🎬 Documentary ⏱ ~15 min

60 Minutes Australia investigates a remote Turkish family whose members walk on all fours — a phenomenon that sparked global scientific debate about human evolution and neurological development.

60 Minutes Reporter: In a remote rural area of Turkey lives a family unlike any other in the world. Several members of the Ulas family walk on all fours — not occasionally, not as children learning to crawl, but as their primary mode of movement through the world.

When scientists first discovered this family in the early 2000s, it sparked one of the most extraordinary and controversial debates in modern science. Were these people providing a window into human evolution? Were they somehow demonstrating what our earliest ancestors looked like as they transitioned from walking on four limbs to standing upright?

Scientist: Some researchers argued that this was evidence of 'devolution' — that these individuals had, in some way, reverted to an ancestral form of movement. But that interpretation was strongly contested. The family's gait is quite different from our primate cousins. They walk on the palms of their hands, not their knuckles, which is unusual in the animal kingdom.

60 Minutes Reporter: The more widely accepted scientific explanation involves the cerebellum — the part of the brain that controls balance and coordination. Medical imaging revealed abnormalities in this region in the affected family members, which make it extremely difficult for them to maintain an upright posture and walk on two legs.

There is also a genetic component. The family carries a rare mutation that affects brain development. This is not a choice — it is a neurological condition. And far from being primitive, it represents an extraordinary human adaptation to a very unusual physical challenge.

Scientist: What is remarkable is not that they walk differently — it is how well they have adapted. They live full lives. They work. They communicate. They are part of a community. The way they move is one small part of who they are.
