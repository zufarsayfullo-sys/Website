Naps: Good for your brain?

B2 🧠 Health 👥 Conversation ⏱ ~6 min

BBC News Review explores new research suggesting that regular short daytime naps may preserve brain size, keep your mind sharp, and reduce the risk of conditions like Alzheimer's disease.

🗣 Neil · Phil (BBC Learning English)
🎬 Watch / Listen

💡 Tip: Try listening without reading the transcript first — then use the vocabulary and transcript to check your understanding.

📚 Key Vocabulary

Learn these words before or after listening

power nap
noun phrase
a short sleep taken during the day, usually to improve alertness and work performance
"She takes a 20-minute power nap after lunch to boost her productivity."
nap
noun/verb
a short sleep, especially during the day
"A quick nap in the afternoon can improve concentration."
cat nap
noun phrase
a very short, light sleep
"He had a cat nap on the sofa before dinner."
snooze
verb/noun
sleep lightly for a short time
"I snoozed for ten minutes before my alarm went off again."
nod off
phrasal verb
fall asleep gradually, especially when you don't intend to
"She nodded off during the long lecture."
sharp
adjective
mentally alert; quick-thinking
"Regular naps could help keep your mind sharp into old age."
hinder
verb
make something more difficult or less effective
"Long naps can hinder health rather than help it."
capacity
noun
the ability to do or produce something; the maximum amount something can hold
"Power naps may help maintain brain capacity as we age."
Alzheimer's
noun
a form of dementia causing progressive memory loss and confusion
"Scientists hope regular naps could reduce the risk of Alzheimer's disease."
obesity
noun
the condition of being dangerously overweight
"Long naps of more than an hour have been linked to a higher risk of obesity."

Neil: Regular short naps during the day might help maintain brain size and capacity as people age. That's according to a new study. Researchers suggest that these naps might reduce the risk of developing conditions like Alzheimer's in later life. This is News Review from BBC Learning English. I'm Neil.

Phil: And I'm Phil. You've been looking at the headlines. What's the vocabulary? We have 'power nap', 'sharp', and 'hinder'.

Neil: Our first headline is: "Power naps linked to greater brain capacity, scientists say." So what is a power nap?

Phil: A power nap is a short sleep taken during the working day in order to be able to do your job better. Anyone can have a nap — but a power nap is specifically associated with improving performance. You might also know 'cat nap' — similar, but not associated with work. And we have other words for sleep, like 'snooze' or 'to nod off'.

Neil: OK, well before our audience nods off! Our second headline: "Taking more naps could help you to stay sharp in later life." What does 'sharp' mean here?

Phil: If you describe someone as sharp, it means they are alert and can think quickly. It's a metaphor — like a sharp knife. And here, regular naps will keep your brain performing well — keep it sharp. Just like how practising your English every day will keep your language skills sharp!

Neil: Our third headline: "Naps could help or hinder your health, depending on their length." This is about a different study — it found that long naps are associated with obesity, while shorter naps can reduce the risk of high blood pressure. The word is 'hinder'.

Phil: To hinder means to make things harder or worse. Here, the headline is saying that some naps — the longer ones — actually make your health worse. So napping is good, but only if you keep it short!
