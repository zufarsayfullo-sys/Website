Why can't we stop eating certain foods?

B2 🥗 Health 🎬 Documentary ⏱ ~10 min

BBC investigates why ultra-processed foods are so hard to resist — and why the answer has less to do with willpower than with the way food is engineered.

🗣 Dr Chris van Tulleken · BBC

Narrator: "Something happened to our food in the mid-seventies to make it irresistible to people." — Dr Chris van Tulleken, NHS doctor and scientist.

Obesity rates remained relatively steady from 1960 to 1975, but then increased sharply across all age groups — in every country at the same time. This global pattern suggests the cause is unlikely to be a simultaneous failure of willpower in millions of people. Instead, something changed in the food system around the mid-1970s that made food harder to resist.

Dr van Tulleken: Food companies have learned to optimize flavour, taste, and texture to increase how much people eat. Soft, easy-to-chew foods bypass the normal satiety signals triggered by chewing — leading to faster consumption and overeating. Some ultra-processed foods are deliberately designed to be light and puffy, to melt in the mouth, so that your brain never quite receives the signal that you are full.

Narrator: Former food industry experts, who now speak openly about their past work, describe how products were tested on focus groups and refined until they triggered the strongest possible response. The goal was not nutrition — it was repeat purchase.

Food industry expert: We knew exactly what we were doing. The science was there. We understood the brain's reward system. We understood that combining fat and sugar in certain ratios created a response that was difficult to stop. We optimized for that.

Narrator: Some researchers now argue that certain ultra-processed foods share characteristics with addictive substances — they trigger compulsive consumption even when people want to stop. The food industry contests this comparison. But the data on obesity, which has risen in parallel with the rise of ultra-processed food, is difficult to explain away.
