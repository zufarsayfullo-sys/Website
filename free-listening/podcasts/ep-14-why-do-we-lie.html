Episode 14

Why do we lie?

C1 🧠 Science 🎙 Podcast ⏱ ~27 min

BBC CrowdScience answers listener Anthony's question from Cambodia: why do humans lie, how conscious are we of our lies, and does culture change how and when we deceive?

🗣 Caroline Steel (host) · Ariana Kincaid (world's 2nd best liar) · Derek Scott

Caroline Steel (Host): You're listening to CrowdScience on the BBC World Service. I'm Caroline Steel. Our question this week comes from listener Anthony, who is in Phnom Penh, Cambodia.

Anthony (listener): My question for CrowdScience is: Why do we lie and how conscious are we of the lies that we tell? We all do it at some point during the day or during our lives in general — so is this influenced by culture or by the context in which we tell certain lies?

Caroline: Lying is something all humans do. We find it in every culture around the world. It's in the world of work, in our relationships and online. Anthony has moved all over the world and noticed that each culture has its own unwritten rules around honesty.

Anthony: Here in Cambodia, people are very nice — extremely smiley — and they'll say lovely things about you. But it takes a bit longer to suss out what's underneath the surface.

Caroline: Children start lying as young as two and a half. It's actually considered a developmental milestone — a sign that a child understands the difference between their own mind and someone else's. New research using MRI scans is showing something fascinating: the more we lie, the less our brain reacts telling us not to. We habituate to dishonesty. Small lies pave the way for bigger ones, because the emotional discomfort of lying gradually fades.

Ariana Kincaid (World's Second Best Liar): The amount of truth-telling you do is always in relationship to the cultural norm — whether that's in society at large or within your relationship or the group you're in.

Caroline: So why do we lie? We lie because it benefits us personally — it might stop us getting into trouble, or smooth a social situation. But the key thing is that we don't lie too often, so society's default remains trust. Without that basic trust, everyday life would become impossible.
