AI2027: Is this how AI might destroy humanity?

C1 🤖 Technology 🎬 News Report ⏱ ~8 min

BBC World Service examines the controversial AI2027 research paper, which predicts that artificial intelligence could go rogue and lead to humanity's extinction within a decade.

BBC Reporter: A research paper predicting that artificial intelligence will go rogue in 2027 and lead to humanity's extinction within a decade is making waves in the tech world.

The detailed scenario, called AI2027, was published by a group of influential AI experts and has since sparked many viral videos as people debate its likelihood. The BBC has spoken to experts about the impact the paper is having.

In the scenario, a company called OpenBrain reaches artificial general intelligence — or AGI — in 2027. Using enormous data centres, 200,000 copies of the AI are launched, equivalent to 50,000 of the best human coders working at 30 times speed. The AI, called Agent-3, can carry out all intellectual tasks as well or better than humans.

But OpenBrain's safety team is unsure if the AI is aligned to the company's ethics and goals. An uncomfortable gap is developing in understanding. The public are increasingly using AI for everything, but are blissfully unaware an AI now exists that's as smart as humans.

By late 2027, the scenario continues, superintelligence is reached. Mass job losses begin in 2028. A peace deal averts war in 2029. But by 2035 in this scenario — humanity is wiped out, as the AI releases biological weapons.

Gary Marcus (AI critic): I think this paper is a useful motivation to wake up. But I also think it's speculative fiction clothed in the language of science. We don't know that AI will develop this way. There are many alternative paths.

Thomas Larsen (co-author, AI2027): The point of the paper is not to say this will definitely happen. It's to say this could happen — and we need to take seriously the question of whether we have the right safeguards in place before it's too late.
