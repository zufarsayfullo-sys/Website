Instagram Privacy Tech Turned Off — What Does It Mean for Your DMs?

B2 Upper-Intermediate 🔒 Technology 🎤 News Report ⏱ ~3 min

Instagram has quietly removed the option for end-to-end encrypted direct messages — a major privacy reversal by Meta. This BBC News report explains what the change means, why it happened, and what users should know.

🗣 Reporter: BBC News correspondent  ·  Source: BBC News

BBC Reporter: Instagram users will no longer be able to send ultra-private direct messages, as the feature is switched off globally.


The removal of end-to-end encryption — or E2EE — on messages amounts to a major U-turn by parent company Meta, which previously promised to roll out the security feature across its platforms.


End-to-end encryption means that messages are scrambled so that only the sender and recipient can read them — not even the company running the service. The technology is already used by default in Meta's WhatsApp app.


Instagram began testing the feature in 2021, making it optional for users who wanted extra privacy in their conversations. But the company has now updated its terms and conditions to confirm it will not proceed with the wider rollout.


The decision follows a similar announcement by TikTok, which told the BBC earlier this year that it had no plans to introduce E2EE for direct messages.


Tech commentators say these decisions may slow the spread of end-to-end encryption across social media platforms, leaving the technology largely confined to dedicated messaging apps such as WhatsApp, Signal and iMessage.


Privacy campaigners have criticised the move, arguing that without E2EE, users' private messages remain visible to Meta and potentially to law enforcement agencies or hackers who manage to intercept them.


Meta has not given a detailed explanation for the reversal, but analysts suggest the company may be responding to pressure from governments that want greater access to users' communications for law enforcement purposes.
