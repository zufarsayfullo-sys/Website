Talking about confidence 💃😎

B1 💬 Everyday English 👥 Conversation ⏱ ~5 min

A natural, friendly conversation about confidence — what it means, when people feel it and when they don't — perfect for practising real-life English vocabulary and expressions.

📚 Key Vocabulary

Learn these words before or after listening

confident
adjective
sure of yourself and your abilities
"She walked into the interview feeling confident and well-prepared."
self-conscious
adjective
uncomfortably aware of yourself and how others see you
"He felt self-conscious speaking in front of the whole class."
fake it till you make it
phrase
act confident even when you don't feel it, until confidence becomes real
"She used the 'fake it till you make it' approach for her first few months at work."
comfort zone
noun phrase
the range of situations where you feel safe and relaxed
"Doing stand-up comedy was way outside my comfort zone."
self-doubt
noun
a lack of confidence in yourself and your abilities
"Even experienced performers sometimes struggle with self-doubt."
assertive
adjective
confident about stating your needs and opinions; not aggressive but firm
"Being assertive at work helped her get her ideas heard."
put yourself out there
phrase
make yourself visible or take risks in social or professional situations
"You have to put yourself out there if you want to make new friends."
overthink
verb
think about something too much, causing worry or hesitation
"I tend to overthink before speaking in meetings."
body language
noun phrase
the way you communicate through your posture, gestures and facial expressions
"Standing up straight can improve your body language and make you seem more confident."
imposter syndrome
noun phrase
the feeling that you don't deserve your success and that others will find you out
"Many successful people admit to experiencing imposter syndrome."

Host 1: So today we're talking about confidence. How confident would you say you are?

Host 2: Honestly, it depends so much on the situation. In some things — like talking to people one-on-one — I feel pretty confident. But put me in front of a large group and I can feel quite self-conscious.

Host 1: Yeah, same. I think most people are like that. It's not like you're either confident or you're not — it's more of a sliding scale, isn't it?

Host 2: Absolutely. And I think confidence can be built. Have you heard the phrase 'fake it till you make it'?

Host 1: Yes! I used to do that a lot when I first started working. I had massive self-doubt but I tried to act confident and eventually it kind of... became real?

Host 2: That's exactly how it works. Your body language actually feeds back into how you feel. If you stand up straight, make eye contact, speak clearly — your brain starts to believe you're confident even if you didn't feel it at the start.

Host 1: One thing I really struggle with is overthinking. Like, before a big meeting or presentation, I'll run through every possible way it could go wrong.

Host 2: I do that too. But I've found that the more you put yourself out there — the more you step outside your comfort zone — the less scary it gets each time.

Host 1: Do you know about imposter syndrome?

Host 2: Yes — that feeling that you don't really deserve to be where you are, and eventually someone's going to figure you out. It's shockingly common, even among very successful people.

Host 1: So I think the key takeaway is: confidence isn't something you either have or you don't. It's a skill you can develop — by doing things that feel uncomfortable, being more assertive, and being a bit kinder to yourself.
