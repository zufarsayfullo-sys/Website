Amazing adventurers

Have you ever dreamt of climbing Mount Everest or visiting Antarctica? Meet some of the 21st century's greatest adventurers.

✍ English Flow·📅 Article·⏱ 5 min read·📊 B1 Easy

Have you ever dreamt of climbing Mount Everest or visiting Antarctica? Every year, thousands of people try to climb the world's highest mountains or walk across continents. In the past, explorerspeople who travel to discover new places had compassestools that show direction (north, south, east, west) and maps, but today's adventurers have satellite phonesphones that work using satellites in space and GPS. They also use their travels to let the world know about climate change.

Amazon adventurer — Ed Stafford from the UK is the first person to walk the length of the Amazon River. He started by a small streama small narrow river in the Andes mountains of Peru and arrived at the river's mouththe place where a river flows into the sea in Brazil, two years and four months later. Snakes, crocodiles and jaguars live in the Amazon, so it's a dangerous place. Ed avoided the big animals, but he was bitten by ants and mosquitoes every day.

Technology was essentialcompletely necessary for Ed. He used a radio to ask local people for food and permissionofficial agreement that you can do something to enter their land. Many of them came to meet him and guide him through the densethick and difficult to move through rainforesta thick forest in tropical areas with lots of rain. As he walked, Ed wrote a bloga regular online diary or article about his daily experiences. Ed used his walk to raise moneycollect money for a charity or cause for environmental charitiesorganisations that help people in need.

A mountain climber — Thousands of climbers, aged between 13 and 80, have been to the top of Everest. Gerlinde Kaltenbrunner from Austria fell in love with climbing as a teenager. Starting with Everest, she has been climbing all the world's fourteen peaksthe tops of mountains over 8,000 metres. To increase the challenge, Gerlinde climbs without using oxygen tankscontainers that hold air for breathing in places with little oxygen. Gerlinde is passionatehaving very strong feelings about something about Nepal and raises money for a charity for poor children and orphanschildren whose parents are dead there.

More than one adventure — Some of today's adventurers go from challenge to challenge. Meagan McGrath from Canada has climbed the highest mountain on each continent and run a long-distance race in the Sahara Desert. But perhaps her most incredible journey was a skiing trip to the South Pole. She pulled a tent and all her food on a sledgea vehicle that slides on snow, often pulled by hand behind her. On the first day, she fell into a glacier and had to be rescuedsaved from a dangerous situation. Many people would have given up, but Meagan decided to carry oncontinue doing something.

Erik Weihenmayer from the United States is another unstoppable adventurer. He's climbed mountains, ridden a bike through the deserts of Morocco and kayakedtravelled in a small narrow boat with a paddle through the Grand Canyon. Amazingly, Erik has been blind since the age of 13. Apart from his travels, he helps people with disabilities to live active lives and takes groups of young blind people on climbing expeditionslong trips for a special purpose, like exploring.

Where Next? Despite new technologies, adventurers still have to live with terrible weather, lack of food and wild animals. Preparation and training are essential, but if you have a sense of adventure, there are continents to cross and hundreds of mountains to climb.
