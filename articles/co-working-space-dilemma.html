You be the judge: should my partner use a co-working space so I can have our flat to myself?

When Xavier moved into Claire's flat, he agreed he wouldn't work there — but would still prefer to WFH. You decide if Claire's right to play the boss.

✍ Interviews by Georgina Lawton · 📅 7 August 2025 · ⏱ 4 min read · 📊 B2 Intermediate

The prosecution: Claire

1Before Xavier moved into my flat, I lived alone for three years. Before he moved in, I said we could live together but that he needed to find somewhere else to work so we weren't on top of each othertoo close to each other in a way that feels uncomfortable or annoying. I asked him to find a co-working space to help us keep a healthy relationship.

2Also, when I wake up, I don't want to speak for a while. Because I work remotelywork from home or another location, not the office for a clienta person or company that pays you for your work who doesn't need me until 1pm, my mornings are my own. Having Xavier in my flat all day every day isn't an option.

3My job is 90 per cent phone calls, so Xavier had to leave. Also, it's my flat, and he was the one who suggested moving in. Luckily, we are good as flatmatespeople who share a flat or house but are not romantic partners, but I like to have different spaces for work, sleep and socialising. Xavier doesn't think like that. He can get up, start work straight awayimmediately, with no delay and then leave the house.

4It took Xavier a while to organise a co-working spacea shared office that people from different companies pay to use. Before that, he was hanging around at home. One day, a month after we started living together, he wanted to take a call at the flat. I said it was fine, but actually it was annoying. Later, we had to have a conversation, and he joined a co-working space right after that.

5I like to talk about things that come up straight away, but Xavier will avoidstay away from something or refuse to deal with it things and hope they'll disappear. Luckily, there are no other issues between us. But I think it's important to have rules.

6Xavier can work from home, but I'd prefer it if he stuck to the routine and came back in the afternoon while I'm working and not when I want to relax alone in the morning. It's important to have separation as a couple.

The defence: Xavier

7About a month after I moved into Claire's flat, she told me I needed to go to a co-working space. We both work remotely, and when I moved in she did mentionsay or talk about something briefly this.

8I'd never been to a co-working space; I'd always worked at home. I'm more productiveable to do or finish a lot of work in a short time that way. I wake up, have a coffee and start work straight away. I like starting early and finishing by 2 or 3pm, and having my own space.

9Claire starts at 1pm, so I thought: great, I'll work in our flat until she needs the office, and pop into a co-working space now and againfrom time to time, occasionally. But then, after about 10 days, she said it wasn't working, that she wanted me to work outside of the flat all the time.

10Claire said she wanted her space in the mornings. She doesn't talk much, but I am a morning person. I didn't like having to find a co-working spot straight away. I needed to work out what I liked and what I wanted out of one. I tried one place, and it was too social, and it affected my productivity.

11Claire is very good at communicating, and I'm glad she told me she wanted me out of the flat. And I have managed to find a better co-working space that is less sociableenjoying being with other people, friendly and talkative.

12In some ways, my working there makes our lives more interesting, because I come home with news about my day. But sometimes I wish I had a bit more freedomthe right or ability to do what you want without being controlled to be in the flat because it's my home, too.
