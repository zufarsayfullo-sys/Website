Happiness

What makes you happy? Read about how to design your life to maximise happiness.

✍ English Flow·📅 Article·⏱ 6 min read·📊 B1 Easy

Do you know what makes you happy or do you just think you know? At first glancewhen you look at something for the first time, these two questions look the same. After all, you know yourself, don't you?

Write a list of all the things that make you happy. Now look at that list and tickmake a small mark to show something is correct or done all the ones that are fun or enjoyable. Probably most of them, right? So, if you spend your time doing all this fun stuffthings that are enjoyable to do (informal), you'll be really happy, won't you?

Well, maybe not. For most people, only doing things for fun isn't enough for real happiness. According to author Paul Dolan, your happiness depends onis decided by; needs a "pleasure–purposethe reason for doing something; meaning balancean equal mix between two things". In order to be happy, you also need activities that give your life purpose.

We usually know if something is enjoyable, but to know what brings meaningthe importance of something and valuehow important or useful something is requires more thought. For example, most people will say that air pilotspeople who fly aeroplanes have jobs with clear purpose. They are responsible forin charge of; having a duty to look after hundreds of people and fly all over the world. But they often have to spend time in boring hotels or stuckunable to move or leave in airports. Just like everyone else, pilots need balance.

There are different ways we can find purpose. Some activities are motivatingmaking you want to do something because they work towards the "greater goodwhat is best for everyone". Or what you do might help a team. Or it might be motivating to see you're making progressmoving forward; getting better at something.

If you're still in education, you can think of your "job" as studying. It's easy to do well in subjects you enjoy. But subjects you don't like are much less motivating. Realistically, society won't benefitget help or advantage from something from you getting an A in a subject you hate. But can you find a way to be part of a study team? The purpose becomes about helping the team.

Now you need to find balance. Paul Dolan believes in "deciding, designing, doing". First decide what brings you pleasure and/or purpose. Then design your life so you do as many of them as possible.

It's easy to make excusesreasons given to avoid doing something not to do things because they're not convenienteasy to use; not causing problems. For example, you might love riding a bike but never have time. But it's up to you to build a routinea regular way of doing things where you can use it to go to school. Some parts of our lives are the result of good or bad luckgood or bad things that happen by chance, but we can still design the parts that are under our control to maximisemake as big or as much as possible happiness.
